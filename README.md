## What "Affordable Colocation" Actually Means in 2026

Here's the thing nobody tells you up front: colocation pricing is a moving target. Industry data from Encoradvisors and DatacenterHawk puts the average wholesale rate somewhere around $86–$110 per kW per month in 2026, with retail colocation landing higher. A dedicated 1 Gbps transit connection alone can run $500–$1,000/month depending on the carrier. So when a provider quotes you "$50 for a 1U," the question isn't whether that number is real — it usually is — it's what's bundled inside it.

**Affordable** in this context usually means three things working together:

- **Low base space cost** — the per-U or per-rack fee, before power and bandwidth.
- **Included, unmetered bandwidth** — so you're not paying per-GB overages the first time your app gets popular.
- **No hidden setup gouging** — or at least a setup fee that's reasonable relative to the monthly run rate.

The providers that consistently win the "affordable" label in Reddit threads, WHTop comparisons, and hosting-advice roundups are the ones that bundle all three. Sharktech sits in that bucket, alongside names like InterServer and a handful of regional players — but Sharktech is the only one in that group throwing in multi-100G redundant networking and always-on DDoS protection across five Tier-grade facilities (Equinix, CoreSite, H5 Data Centers, and CrownCastle buildings) for the same starting price.
